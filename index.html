The variety

Why we only grow Red Russian.

Red Russian is a hardneck garlic with purple-tinged skin and a rich, complex flavour that is strong without being overwhelming. It handles cold Canadian winters, resists disease, and stores well once properly cured.

The growing year

From one clove to a full bulb.

Garlic in Canada follows the seasons. Each harvest gives us the cloves we plant again in the fall.

  1. Fall

    Planting

    Single cloves go into the soil before winter.

  2. Winter

    Rooting

    Under straw mulch, the cloves build strong roots.

  3. Spring

    Emerging

    Green shoots push up through the soil.

  4. Early summer

    Scapes

    Curling flower stalks, a harvest of their own.

  5. July – August

    Harvest

    Bulbs are lifted from the field by hand.

  6. After harvest

    Curing

    Bulbs dry slowly for flavour and long storage.

Why choose Red Russian garlic over other varieties?

Red Russian stands out for its bold flavour, purple-tinged skin and ability to thrive in cold Canadian climates.

Read more

Compared to commonly imported softneck varieties from China or California, Red Russian garlic delivers a richer, more complex taste with fewer, larger cloves that are easier to peel and use in cooking.

It adapts well to harsh winters and has high disease resistance. Because it is grown locally, it is harvested fresh, without being bleached or treated with preservatives, unlike mass-produced imports that often travel thousands of kilometres before reaching your kitchen.

Buying locally grown garlic supports Canadian farmers, reduces your environmental impact and gives you a fresher, more nutritious product.

What makes Red Russian garlic ideal for Canadian gardens?

Few varieties match the reliability of Red Russian in a Canadian garden. Its vigorous roots and winter hardiness let it thrive in BC.

Read more

Red Russian produces 4–8 large, flavourful cloves per bulb, which are easier to plant, peel and cook with than smaller, densely packed softneck varieties. It also yields garlic scapes in early summer, an extra harvest for home gardeners and commercial growers.

It is less prone to disease, handles frost better and stores well when cured properly. The flavour is strong but not overwhelming, which makes it a staple in rustic and gourmet dishes.

Growing or buying Red Russian locally also helps protect biodiversity, reduce pesticide use and build a sustainable food economy in Canada.

How is garlic grown in Canada?

Canadian garlic farming follows the seasons: planted in fall, overwintered under straw, harvested in mid to late summer.

Read more

Cloves are tucked into the soil before winter and insulated with straw mulch, so they establish strong roots and emerge vigorously in spring. By July or August the bulbs are harvested, cured and prepared for sale or storage.

The cooler climate in provinces like British Columbia and Ontario suits hardneck varieties like Red Russian, which produce scapes in early summer and develop richer flavour over the long season.

Canadian garlic is often grown on smaller farms using sustainable, low-intervention methods. That means fewer chemicals, healthier soil and garlic that keeps more of its natural nutrients and flavour.
